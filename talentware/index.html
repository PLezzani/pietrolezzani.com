Talentware

From a reactive roadmap to a product strategy

Talentware built on customer feedback. Put that way it sounds like a virtue, which is exactly why the problem stays invisible for so long: everything gets built and nothing gets chosen, the roadmap is written by whatever request came in last, and at the end of the quarter nobody can say which of the things released actually mattered.

There were no design standards, and there was no way to test an idea before releasing it. Every feature was a bet whose outcome was discovered later, on the market, where correcting costs the most. The bottleneck was not the ability to design screens. It was the absence of a criterion for deciding which ones to design.

That is what we worked on, together and in the order it was needed. Design governance, meaning who decides what, on what criterion and at what point. A product strategy that let the roadmap hold up against customer requests without refusing them on instinct. A design system, to make repeatable what until then was rebuilt every time.

During the engagement Talentware went from roughly 10 to 45 customers. Design did not bring them in. Sales did. The point is that the product carried that multiplication without having to be rewritten, and that the features released in the meantime were chosen rather than absorbed. That is exactly what we had built for.
