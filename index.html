Three situations I get called into

Your roadmap belongs to your customers.

Every quarter closes with a list of releases and no way to say which of them mattered, and you are the one who has to explain that to the board.

You moved upmarket and the product stayed behind.

Onboarding drags, and each new feature costs more than the last because it is being added to a structure that was never meant to receive it.

The strategy is approved and the product does not show it.

The internal team is full, and the vendor building the product executes the brief rather than questioning it.

A product on its own does not make a company successful. What gets decided around it does.

Two ways to start

Product audit and roadmap. Two to three weeks.

You end with your problems ranked by their effect on the number you are measured on, and a six month roadmap where every initiative carries a success criterion and a metric. Execution, design system and primary research are not included.

Redesign of one critical area. Four to six weeks, quoted phase by phase.

The area you already know is costing you. The final phase hands engineering the requirements, so the decisions arrive intact instead of being reinterpreted.

Engagements start at €8,000.

Strategy consulting on one side of the table, product design on the other

Product decisions rarely fail in the room where they are taken. They fail on the way to the screen, because the person who frames the business case and the person who designs the flow are almost never the same one.

Two and a half years at Bain & Company, Vector practice, then independent since January 2024. That is why an audit here ends with a roadmap a board can read and requirements engineering can build from.

None of the three projects above was done alone, and that is the point. The results came from clients who brought the people who decide into the room and owned each decision, rather than signing off on someone else's. That is the way of working on offer here, and it is where the impact comes from.
