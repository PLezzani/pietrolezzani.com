Motion

Text arrives rather than being found in place: 18px of rise and a fade over 1100ms, once, on a cubic ease-out. The hero plays on load, everything else when it enters the view. Lines that belong together are staggered by 140ms. The curve is chosen so the duration is felt: an exponential ease-out spends its budget in the first third, so lengthening it reads as a pause rather than as slower. Nothing is tied to scroll position, nothing is pinned, nothing repeats. Under reduced motion every reveal is skipped, and the hidden state always carries a delayed keyframe that reveals it without script, so a failed file can never leave the page wordless.

Inverse ground

For long reading passages, the page inverts to cream with black text. Sustained legibility beats atmosphere when a case study runs long.

Plan steps

  1. A 30-45 minute call, 3 questions: the deadline your team cannot carry, who decides and on what budget, what happens if nothing changes.

  2. A one page proposal within 48 hours: scope, duration, price, what is excluded.

  3. Work starts on a written scope, never on a verbal yes.

Do's and don'ts

✓ Do

  • Reserve the accent for what has been earned: a proof, a label, a hover. One blue event per screen.
  • Keep body measure near 34em, even when the viewport allows more.
  • Keep the densest and the airiest section far apart in density, several times over. Even spacing everywhere is what makes a page read flat.
  • Let a section change ground when it needs to feel different, instead of reaching for a border or a fill.
  • Set claims that carry a number in the proof line, and only when the number is verifiable.
  • Say a fact once. If a paragraph introduces a step and the step repeats it, the paragraph goes and the step keeps the detail.
  • Use display type for statements, Inter for anything a reader works through.
  • While an image is not available, leave a dashed slot with a note on the subject. A wrong image is worse than a marked gap.

✗ Don't

  • Never add a shadow, a glow, or a blurred layer.
  • Never hide text behind a state that only script can undo, and never tie motion to scroll position.
  • Never introduce a grey that is not the cream stepped down, or a second accent hue.
  • Never let body text stay at 500 once it moves onto the cream ground, footer included. Step it down to 400.
  • Never set an argument above 88px: past that a sentence stops being read and starts being chanted.
  • Never place a second call to action on a page.
  • Never use stock photography, icon sets, or a gradient.
  • Never place a section label below the title it labels, and never show a borrowed logo in its own brand colours.
  • Never arrange mockups as a gallery. Images show the decision, not the craft.
